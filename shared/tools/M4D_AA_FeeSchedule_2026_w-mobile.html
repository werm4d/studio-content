Frequently Asked Questions
What does the architecture fee cover?
"Our fee covers everything from day one through construction closeout — schematic design, full permit drawings, bidding support, and regular site visits during construction. What’s separate is the engineering: structural, MEP, and civil are their own firms, and we pass those through at cost plus ten percent. Before we sign anything, I’ll give you a complete picture of what those subconsultant fees are likely to run so there are no surprises when the full design budget lands."
Does the fee include MEP and engineering?
"Good question — and an important one to clarify up front. Every industry benchmark you’ll find, including what Delaware publishes for state A&E contracts, is architecture-only. MEP, structural, and civil are separate design firms we coordinate but they bill independently. On a project like yours, figure the full design team runs somewhere between ten and eighteen percent of construction cost total. Our architecture fee is the largest piece; engineering is on top of that. Let me walk you through the breakdown."
Why is the total design cost higher than the quoted fee?
"That’s a common disconnect and I’m glad you asked. The eight percent in the proposal is our architecture fee — it’s what every state benchmark is based on and it’s how architects quote. But a project also needs engineers: structural for the framing, MEP for the mechanical and electrical systems, civil for the site. Those are separate firms with separate fees. We manage them all — one point of contact — but their fees are itemized. The total design investment is typically eleven to sixteen percent all-in."
Can the fee be negotiated?
"Here’s where I can move and where I can’t. Our architecture fee is benchmarked against what the State of Delaware publishes for public A&E contracts — that’s the floor, and going below it means cutting scope, not cutting margin. What I can do is structure phased billing so early phases are a smaller upfront commitment. On the engineering side, those are pass-throughs at cost — I can’t discount them, but I can help you scope them tightly."
Why hire M4D over a cheaper firm?
"Ask them one question: have any of their principals ever sat on Wilmington’s Board of Standards and Appeals or served as Acting Director of L&I? Because ours have. Trevor and Leo have 60 combined years inside the very department that inspects your building. They know what the inspectors look for because they trained them. The fee difference typically pays for itself in the first AHJ comment cycle we prevent."
How does the Forward Home system affect fees?
"The prototype plan is a fixed fee — not a percentage. You’re buying a permit-ready plan set, not a custom design from scratch, so the architecture fee is compressed significantly. What you still need is site engineering — structural review of the panel connections, MEP for the systems, civil for grading and utilities. Those run a few thousand dollars and are quoted separately. Total design cost on a Forward Home unit is well below conventional because the architecture work is mostly already done."
Standard Fee Disclosure Language — Ready to Insert in Proposals
Insert in all fee proposals — both firms
"Professional fees for architectural services are proposed as a percentage of the total construction cost of the project and cover all basic services as defined in AIA Document B101-2017, including schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding and negotiation support, and construction administration. These fees are consistent with the Delaware Office of Management and Budget Division of Facilities Management A&E Fee Schedule (OMB22131) and align with 2026 Mid-Atlantic industry benchmarks per the Siana national architecture fee report.

Engineering subconsultant fees — including structural, mechanical/electrical/plumbing (MEP), and civil engineering — are not included in the architectural fee and will be itemized separately as reimbursable expenses at cost plus ten percent (10%) coordination overhead. A preliminary engineering fee estimate will be provided prior to contract execution."
