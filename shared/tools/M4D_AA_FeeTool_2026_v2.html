A drafting service produces drawings; a licensed architect takes legal responsibility for them. The license carries code compliance, life safety, engineering coordination, and a stamp AHJs, lenders, and grant programs recognize. The apparent 1.5–3% drafting fee excludes engineering, code analysis, and permit responsibility — the owner buys those separately and carries the risk in between.
Lean permit sets work when scope is simple enough that reduced documentation doesn't create construction risk. Above $650K new construction or $500K renovation, coordination complexity requires the full Option A scope — the ceiling protects the client as much as the firm.
Step 1 is a fixed $750–$1,500 code and zoning review. If you proceed to Step 2 permit drawings within 90 days, the Step 1 fee credits in full. If the review reveals a variance or pre-application meeting is needed, Step 1 converts to hourly before proceeding.
In Option A, structural and MEP are included via M4D's preferred consultant model for standard projects — one contract, no separate engineering invoices without written authorization. Complex or technical projects carve structural/MEP out at cost + 10%, confirmed in writing at proposal. Civil, survey, and geotechnical are always owner-direct in every option.
Fixed-fee predevelopment packages that answer go/no-go before design fees are committed: P1 site + program screening ($4,000), P2 financial + regulatory analysis ($8,000), P3 funder + partner readiness ($12,000). Full sequence $24,000. Separate from the A&E fee; an M4D service regardless of which firm holds the design contract.
Pre-inspection observation by a former code official before the AHJ inspection — Trevor Knight (ICC CBO) or Leo Lynch (former L&I Director). Catching one failed inspection avoids re-inspection fees, schedule loss, and trade re-mobilization; it typically pays for itself on the first visit. M4D-only service.
