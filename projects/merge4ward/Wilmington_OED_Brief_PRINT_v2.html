Section 1 — Why This Meeting Matters
A Documented Cost-Credibility Gap. A Working Solution. A 14-Day Decision Window.

The City of Wilmington has a quiet but well-documented predevelopment cost-credibility problem that affects every funded project the Office of Economic Development touches. The A&E Feasibility Assistance grant, the Strategic Fund, the DDD rebate, and the Property Tax Abatement program all make commitments downstream against early-stage construction estimates of varying — and unverified — quality. When those estimates collapse during construction documentation or after permit, the City has already absorbed program cost and lost the project.

Merge 4 Design and M4Di have spent the last eighteen months building the methodology, the platform, and the local partnerships that close that gap. We arrive at this meeting with one coherent strategy, four entry points, and zero requests for new City appropriation. Every ask routes through OED programs that already exist.

What Wilmington OED Receives
A coordinated $4M federal grant push — two applications, one City letter — with measurable Year-3 wage-tax return inside the Strategic Fund's recoup window
An independent, architect-led cost-verification standard for the A&E Feasibility Assistance grant — protecting every downstream OED program decision
A vacant Wilmington building put back into productive use as a multi-firm professional services anchor — three named brands at one address
A documented economic development case study: City programs stacked correctly on a tenant who actually uses them
A growing proprietary regional cost benchmark dataset (the M4Di ALDDB) that becomes the City's own standing reference for what construction in Wilmington really costs
What the State of Delaware Wins Alongside
First-of-state CLT/SIP hybrid manufacturing operation in Delaware — the Claymont pilot is the foundation case
DEDO Strategic Fund leverage at the Port — manufacturing jobs paying $22–$34/hr against state grants of $250K–$1M
EPA Brownfields and EDA Public Works alignment on Site A scenarios — federal capital flowing through Wilmington toward state economic development priorities
L.E.E.P. workforce training pipeline — 80–120 Delaware-resident graduates per cycle by Year 3 across CLT/SIP production roles
A regional predevelopment cost intelligence standard headquartered in Wilmington, with the City as its first municipal partner

Section 4 — Ask 2
Adopt the M4Di OI Tool as OED's Cost Verification Standard

The DBE A&E Feasibility Assistance program offers a 50% match up to $15,000 to enumerate construction costs for Wilmington projects. The program's structural design is correct. What it currently lacks — and what M4Di provides — is a standardized, independent, regionally calibrated verification layer that protects every downstream OED commitment.

Today's Workflow Risk Profile What's Missing
DBE applicant secures A&E firm. 50% match up to $15K. Estimate produced. Variable quality. Methodology unknown. Sometimes produced by the GC who would build the project. Independent verification
Strategic Fund / DDD / Property Tax Abatement decisions made downstream. Decisions stack on the same unverified estimate. Regional calibration
Project enters CD phase. Real bids arrive. Budget collapses on a meaningful share of community-development projects nationally. A growing benchmark dataset
WITH M4Di OI TOOL VERIFICATION Independent. Standardized. Methodology documented. All three resolved
M4Di does not replace the A&E firm or the existing program. It adds a parallel, independent verification layer at $3,500–$6,500 per project — well below the $15,000 program ceiling.
Pilot Structure — FY26
3–5 Projects. One Quarter to Prove the Model.
Per project: Independent Gate 0 ROM with regional calibration narrative, gate report, and reconciliation against the applicant's submitted estimate. Two-page summary suitable for OED program file.

Cost to OED: $3,500–$6,500 per engagement.

Outputs: Standardized cost reports. Aggregate annual benchmark data for OED's internal use. Quarterly review.

Year 2: Convert pilot to standing OED engagement. Add Strategic Fund and DDD applications. M4Di publishes the Wilmington Predevelopment Cost Index annually.
The Framing That Will Land
The A&E Feasibility Assistance program is the right idea. M4Di is the natural infrastructure under it. FICO did not replace mortgage underwriting — it standardized the credit signal that mortgage underwriting depended on. M4Di does the same thing for predevelopment cost. OED becomes the first municipality in the Mid-Atlantic to formalize a cost-credibility standard for community development projects — and the precedent it sets is exportable to every peer city in the region.

The City of Wilmington Strategic Fund recoups incentive expenditures within two to three years through wage taxes from new or retained jobs. Horizon Item 1 · Anchor / Signature Project
Riverfront East, Parcel B — A First-of-State CLT/SIP Public Pavilion
RDC's Parcel B sits at the bend of the Christina River, eminently visible from Riverfront West, and is described in the Riverfront East public materials as "an anchor point at the bend in the river" and "a chance for striking retail presence — or whatever your vision holds." The 0.19-acre parcel with a 3,019 SF parcel limit is well-matched to a single-volume CLT/SIP timber pavilion.
Our concept — and we want to underscore that this is a concept stage, not a proposal — is an open-air farmers market and entertainment pavilion built with Merge4Ward-produced CLT structural panels and SIP roof assemblies. A first-of-state demonstration of regional sustainable timber manufacturing in a civic, public-facing form. It would visibly mark Wilmington as the East Coast's earliest mover on regional CLT supply, while delivering a permanent public amenity at the most visible point on Riverfront East.
Parcel: 0.19 acres, 3,019 SF parcel limit, 109 ft frontage
Owner / Steward: Riverfront Development Corporation of Delaware

Horizon Item 2 · Specific HQ Candidate
1601 Jessup Street — A Brandywine HQ Candidate, Already Identified
In addition to the general HQ ask in Section 6, we have identified a specific candidate property worth OED's evaluation. 1601 Jessup Street (Parcel #2602930259) is a 0.15-acre Brandywine peninsula site with an existing brick light-manufacturing building (~8,706 SF + secondary structures), ample asphalt parking already in place, and existing perimeter security fencing.
The site offers downstream views of the Wilmington skyline — making it an unusually well-suited client-facing HQ and marketing lounge for M4D, Aubyn Architecture, M4Di, and Merge4Ward. The architectural intervention required is modest: open the riverside elevation, frame the view, refresh the interior to studio + investor lounge + M4Di demo configuration. The brick industrial character is the asset, not the obstacle.
Parcel: 0.15 acres, 30 ft frontage × 89.30 ft depth, ~8,700 SF main building
Zoning: Currently classified Light Manufacturing — Waterfront Res/Comm overlay; City Chapter 48 zoning to be confirmed
Ask of OED (future): Confirm ownership status and any City interest. Evaluate as Ask 4 candidate against Strategic Fund + Property Tax Abatement stack.
