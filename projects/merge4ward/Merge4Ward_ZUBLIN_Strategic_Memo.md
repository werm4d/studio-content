## 1. Who ZÜBLIN Timber Is

ZÜBLIN Timber GmbH is a wholly-owned subsidiary of STRABAG SE — the Austrian construction multinational with roughly €17B in annual revenue and 73,000 employees, one of the largest construction firms in Europe. ZÜBLIN Timber operates from Aichach, Germany with branch offices in Leutkirch, Gaildorf, Neu-Ulm, Stuttgart, and Berlin. They are part of STRABAG's publicly stated commitment to be climate-neutral by 2040.

**Their product line:**

- **LENO® Cross Laminated Timber** — solid CLT panels up to 4.80 m × 20 m (15.7' × 65.6'), thicknesses 60–320 mm, ETA-10/0241 European Technical Assessment
- **LENO®-ADD** — prefab wall panels built on LENO® CLT base with insulation, cladding, and interior finishes installed in factory; either unfinished, unfinished with substructure, or fully finished including wood façade
- **MOLENO timber-hybrid system** — extensively prefabricated LENO® CLT external walls combined with wide-span concrete floors; designed for serial/standardized residential construction
- **LENO®-HYBRID** — CLT panels with substituted high-stress layers (LVL or OSB instead of solid lumber) for large-span or point-supported conditions
- **LENO®-Surfaces** — finishing variants including industrial, industrial-visual, Nordic, fineline, stain/UV protection, gypsum-clad, weather-membrane-coated
- **LENO®-3D** — uniaxially or biaxially curved CLT, radii ≥ 9 m, dimensions 4.50 m × 14.50 m, ETA-10/0241 — a capability no US producer currently offers
- **Stressed-skin panels** — high-performance ribbed panels for spans up to 20 m, GLT-CLT combinations
- **Laminated veneer lumber components** — large-format reglued/pressure-impregnated LVL for façade, columns, beams, and outdoor structural use
- **Tools and joining technology** — column supports, tension anchors, NHT beam connectors, screw connectors, MGA screw foundations, sold under the zimmereibedarf.com brand

**Their service stack:**

- Consulting (pre-project advisory)
- Structural pre-analysis
- Construction proposals
- Structural design and workshop planning
- Element planning (translation of architectural/structural drawings into element-level shop drawings)
- High-quality production with CNC + robot + skilled-employee manual trimming
- On-time delivery with special transports
- Project support throughout the construction phase

**Their target customer language:** the brochure explicitly identifies "Carpenter, Timber Builder, Architect, Engineer" as their primary customers — Craig is two of those four directly. They market to design and construction professionals, not to general contractors or owners. The cultural framing is "From Person to Person — Personal and Authentic" and "We build on your design and support you in the process." This suggests partnership openness rather than commodity supplier posture.

**Their US presence:** none visible in the brochure. No US office, no US subsidiary, no US ESR or US ICC-ES evaluation cited for any LENO® product. All product approvals are European (ETA-10/0241).

## 2. Outreach Question — Should Merge4Ward Reach Out Now?

### 2.1 Why a "yes" is plausible

Three specific reasons make this more than a long shot.

First, the size mismatch cuts both ways. Yes, STRABAG is a top-10 European construction firm and Merge4Ward is a forming five-partner LLC. But STRABAG's stated climate-neutral-by-2040 commitment requires *something* to happen in North America, and ZÜBLIN's brochure explicitly invites partnership ("We build on your design and support you in the process"). Their North American presence is currently zero. If they intend to enter the US market — and the climate commitment plus the brochure framing suggests they are at least open to it — then a US partnership conversation is genuinely interesting to them, not a nuisance.

Second, the technical complementarity is real. ZÜBLIN has twenty years of European CLT/timber-hybrid manufacturing maturity, ETA-approved product lines, and a service model that already handles the architect/engineer/builder customer they want to serve. Merge4Ward has Mid-Atlantic site access, federal/state grant alignment (EDA, NMTC, DEDO, EPA Brownfields), municipal alignment (Wilmington OED), and a workforce pipeline through LEEP DE. The complementarity is asymmetric but coherent.

Third, the precedent exists. Stora Enso (Finnish CLT) has multiple US licensing and supply partnerships. Lendlease (Australian) has entered US modular through partnership rather than greenfield. KLH (Austrian CLT) supplies several US project pipelines through partner-distributors. The European-CLT-firm-partners-with-US-market-entrant pattern is established.

### 2.2 Why a "yes — but not yet" is the right answer

The credibility weight of an outreach today is light. Merge4Ward at this writing has:

- **No signed MOU between the five partners** (David, Trevor, Leo, Travis, Gwen). Per the project record, attorney conversations are in progress but nothing is fully executed.
- **No site.** Two scenarios under evaluation per the OED brief — Site A existing-industrial brownfield (4–6 acres) and Site B City-controlled Port parcel (1–2 acres) — but no letter of intent on either.
- **No first production run.** The Claymont CLT/SIP hybrid pilot is in foundation-only permit phase; panels arrive May 27 from Acme (SIPs) and Panelwrights (CLT). Merge4Ward as a manufacturing entity is not yet operational; it is a customer of those vendors for this first project.
- **No closed grant.** The Wells Fargo/Enterprise Housing Affordability Breakthrough Challenge applications (Merge4Ward in Design & Construction; M4Di OI Tool in Service & Delivery via CB CDC) are pending May 15, 2026 submission with decisions later in the cycle.
- **No US-market pricing model.** Merge4Ward has not yet manufactured a panel and therefore has no actual unit economics to discuss with a sophisticated counterparty.

A partnership inquiry from Merge4Ward today reads as "interesting concept, no track record." That is not a fatal weakness, but it does mean the conversation lands in ZÜBLIN's "explore later, low priority" bin rather than their active business development pipeline. The same email sent twelve months from now — with a closed grant, a leased site, the Claymont units complete, and the first M4D follow-on project under contract — lands very differently.

### 2.3 The carve-out — Riverfront East Parcel B

There is one scenario where reaching out earlier is correct: if Riverfront East Parcel B advances to a real conversation with the Riverfront Development Corporation and the open-air farmers market and entertainment pavilion concept moves toward design.

**LENO®-3D curved CLT is uniquely suited to a public pavilion.** No US manufacturer produces curved structural CLT. ZÜBLIN's LENO®-3D capability — radii ≥ 9 m, dimensions up to 4.50 m × 14.50 m, biaxially curved if needed — is a marquee-project capability that would make a Wilmington pavilion architecturally distinct from anything else built on the East Coast. That is the kind of project a European division division flies to Wilmington for, because it lets them market the project globally as proof-of-concept for their North American capability.

If Parcel B advances, the outreach is project-specific rather than partnership-general — "we have a public pavilion concept on the Christina River that needs your specific 3D-curved-CLT capability, and we are positioning Merge4Ward as the local fabrication and delivery partner." That framing is concrete enough to land on a real desk at ZÜBLIN. It is also exactly the kind of marquee anchor project that a forming Phase-1 manufacturing LLC needs to have credibility for the bigger conversation later.

### 2.4 Recommended outreach sequence

**Now (April–May 2026):** No outreach. Focus on Wells Fargo/Enterprise grant submission, Claymont production start, Merge4Ward LLC formation, and OED follow-up on site identification.

**Q3 2026 (post-Wells Fargo decision):** If Merge4Ward is funded or has a credible alternative grant path, send an introductory email to ZÜBLIN Timber's bauelemente team (`timber-bauelemente@zueblin.de`) and to STRABAG North America (if a contact can be found). Frame as "Mid-Atlantic CLT/SIP manufacturing initiative, Wilmington Delaware, exploring technology and delivery partnerships for North American market entry." Attach the closed grant award and the OED endorsement letter as credibility evidence. Request a 30-minute introductory video call.

**Q4 2026 (post-Claymont production):** If Parcel B has advanced, parallel outreach for the pavilion-specific project conversation. Frame as project-driven inquiry, attach the Parcel B concept package from the OED brief Section 9, and request a feasibility discussion for LENO®-3D fabrication and delivery to a Wilmington site.

**2027:** If both conversations advance, the structure becomes an evaluation of (a) technology licensing / technical-services agreement, (b) supply partnership for marquee projects requiring capabilities Merge4Ward doesn't have in-house, or (c) joint venture for a Mid-Atlantic regional facility under STRABAG's North American expansion umbrella. Each option requires its own negotiation; none is a foregone conclusion.

## 3. Model-Emulation Question — Is ZÜBLIN's Model a Good Template?

### 3.1 What ZÜBLIN actually is

The strategic insight from reading their brochure carefully: **ZÜBLIN Timber is not a CLT factory.** It is a vertically-integrated timber-construction technology platform with the factory as one node among several.

Look at what they sell. They sell technology (multiple LENO® product lines). They sell design support (consulting, structural pre-analysis, element planning). They sell production (CNC and robot manufacturing, manual trimming, custom finishing). They sell delivery (special transports, project-phase support). They sell connectors (their own brand of column supports, tension anchors, beam connectors, screw foundations through zimmereibedarf.com). They sell signature capabilities (LENO®-3D curved CLT for marquee architectural projects). The factory is the anchor, but the revenue mix is broader than a factory's revenue mix.

Compare to most US CLT producers — SmartLam, Nordic Structures, Mercer, Vaagen Brothers, Element5, Sterling, Freres. They are commodity panel manufacturers. They sell panels by the cubic meter and the architect/engineer/contractor figures out everything else. Their margin is squeezed by panel-price competition and by the fact that the customer has to assemble the rest of the project delivery stack from independent vendors. They are stuck at the bottom of the value chain.

ZÜBLIN sits at the *top* of the value chain — selling the project-delivery outcome with manufacturing in service of it. That is a much higher-margin, much more defensible position.

### 3.2 What this means for Merge4Ward's strategic shape

The October 2025 strategy document framed Merge4Ward as a Phase-1 manufacturing LLC that converts to a worker cooperative in Year 3-4, with M4D as a primary customer and supply chain ties to other regional buyers. That model is a **commodity manufacturer** on a US-producer template. It is correct as a Phase 1 starting point — you have to make panels reliably before you sell anything else — but it leaves the larger strategic position on the table.

The ZÜBLIN model suggests a stronger Phase-2 and Phase-3 alternative: Merge4Ward as a **vertically-integrated timber project delivery firm** that happens to operate a manufacturing facility. Same factory, same workforce training, same site — but the product *catalog* and the service *menu* expand over time to capture the higher-margin work above the panel itself.

### 3.3 What's directly importable from ZÜBLIN's catalog

Walking through ZÜBLIN's product lineup with Merge4Ward's plans in mind:

**LENO®-ADD parallels what M4D's existing capability could productize.** Pre-finished prefab wall panels with insulation, cladding, and interior finishes built in factory. The Claymont pilot is already a small-scale version of this — CLT/SIP envelope panels with detailing baked in via Aubyn's drawings. Merge4Ward Phase 2 could productize this as a recurring offering: "Merge4Ward Wall — fully-finished load-bearing prefab wall panel, factory-trimmed for your specific project, delivered ready to install." This is significantly higher-margin than selling raw CLT or raw SIP panels.

**MOLENO timber-hybrid maps directly onto Mid-Atlantic affordable housing.** The MOLENO concept — extensively prefabricated CLT exterior walls combined with wide-span concrete floors — is the model that solves the structural-grid problem in 4-story affordable housing. Most US affordable housing in this typology is currently wood-framed because concrete floors are expensive and grid-constrained. MOLENO shows that CLT walls plus *less* concrete (wide-span floors instead of grid floors) can be cost-competitive while opening up unit-plan flexibility. For future Wilmington Housing Authority projects (Lobdel Avenue, the next WHA pipeline), a MOLENO-equivalent system is directly applicable. **Merge4Ward Phase 3 product line: "MOLENO-equivalent multi-story residential system, Mid-Atlantic edition."**

**The connectors and tools business is a hidden margin line.** ZÜBLIN sells Rothoblaas-equivalent products (column supports, tension anchors, NHT beam connectors, screw connectors, MGA screw foundations) under their own zimmereibedarf.com brand. Connector and fastener distribution is a 30–40% margin business. If Merge4Ward distributes US-market CLT/SIP connectors — even just as a reseller to projects that don't buy panels from Merge4Ward — that is a parallel revenue line that does not require additional manufacturing capacity to scale. This is also where the existing Rothoblaas TCP300 question on the Claymont A-502 detail (US ESR verification status) intersects: **if Rothoblaas does not have US ESR coverage for their TCP300 connector, Merge4Ward could become the regional distributor that bridges that gap** by providing ICC-ES-equivalent engineering judgment letters for projects using the connector. That's a product-management-and-engineering-services business sitting on top of the manufacturing business.

**LENO®-3D curved CLT is a Year-5+ signature capability.** Adding a 3D press to the manufacturing facility is a major capital investment ($1.5M+ for the press alone) and only makes sense once standard panel production is at Phase 1 maturity and the marquee-project pipeline justifies the equipment. But the strategic position is real — the only US producer of curved structural CLT becomes the single source for every architecturally-ambitious public building in the Mid-Atlantic region. That earns press, awards, AIA recognition, and a fundamentally different inbound-call profile than a commodity manufacturer.

### 3.4 The integration question — SIPs, off-site stick framing, 3D concrete printing

The question Craig actually asked included whether the ZÜBLIN model accommodates SIPs, off-site stick framing, and 3D concrete printed components. Each deserves a different answer.

**SIPs fit cleanly under the model.** ZÜBLIN does not sell SIPs explicitly because the European market shifted past SIPs toward CLT-based prefab walls (LENO®-ADD). In the US, SIPs remain a major residential and small-commercial product category, and Acme Panel SIPs are already part of the Claymont project supply chain. The right framing for Merge4Ward: SIPs and CLT-prefab walls are *parallel offerings* under one delivery platform. Light projects (single-family residential, small multifamily, light commercial) get SIPs through a continued Acme Panel distribution relationship. Heavier projects (multi-story residential, schools, commercial) get CLT-based LENO®-ADD-equivalent walls fabricated by Merge4Ward. The customer chooses based on project requirements; Merge4Ward delivers either.

**Off-site stick framing fits less cleanly but is achievable.** ZÜBLIN does not do stick framing because European wood construction is largely past that. In the US, stick framing remains roughly 90% of residential construction and is unlikely to disappear from the market in any reasonable horizon. Merge4Ward could offer panelized off-site stick-framing as a *transition product* for projects whose budgets do not support CLT/SIP — same crew, same factory, same delivery logistics, just lighter material. Bensonwood in New Hampshire built a roughly $40M-revenue business on this model. The risk is **mission dilution.** If Merge4Ward becomes "the panelized framing company" instead of "the CLT/SIP timber company", the workforce training story weakens, the carbon-footprint story weakens, and the grant-eligibility story weakens — all three are arguments for *advanced* sustainable timber, not for traditional stick framing in a different shape. So the recommendation is yes, achievable, but it must be a clearly-named secondary product line marketed as a transition offering, not the primary brand. Something like "Merge4Ward Bridge — panelized stick-framing for projects in transition to advanced timber systems."

**3D concrete printing is a different category.** Different material (concrete, not wood), different equipment (gantry-style printer, not CLT press), different technical staff (concrete technologists, not timber engineers), different customer base (foundations, retaining walls, hardscape, possibly façade panels). Putting this under the Merge4Ward roof creates a category mistake — the sustainable-timber story dilutes, the workforce training pipeline splits between two unrelated trades, and the equipment-depreciation profiles don't align. The **right way to integrate it is as a separate sister entity** — Merge4Ward Print LLC or similar — sharing the site (visible on the same campus to clients touring the operation), sharing the workforce training pipeline at the entry level (general construction technology fundamentals before tracks split), and sharing the M4D customer base, but with its own equipment, its own learning curve, and its own P&L. The argument for putting it on the same campus is real (clients touring see a "complete sustainable construction stack"). The argument for separating the entities is also real (3D concrete printing is currently money-losing in most US installations and shouldn't drag down a profitable timber operation's balance sheet during the learning years). **Recommendation: same site, separate entity, Phase 3 timing once Merge4Ward Phase 1 is profitable.**

### 3.5 What this implies for Phase 1 structure

The implication for the *current* Phase 1 work — the Merge4Ward LLC operating agreement, the site selection, the equipment specification — is that decisions made now should leave room for the Phase 2 and Phase 3 expansion.

Specifically:

The site should accommodate the larger build-out. Phase 1 is 30,000–45,000 SF for SIP press operations, CLT staging and cutting, and LEEP workforce training. Phase 2 (LENO®-ADD-equivalent prefab walls) needs additional floor space for finishing operations, paint booth, glazing installation, and clad-panel storage. Phase 3 (LENO®-3D curved CLT plus the connector distribution business plus the sister concrete-printing operation) needs additional acreage. **The site shortlist should evaluate ultimate footprint at 8–12 acres, not just the Phase 1 footprint of 1–6 acres.** Site A's 4–6 acres with brownfield is the right Phase 1 site; Site B's 1–2 acres is only viable if it is combined with adjacent expansion parcels.

The equipment specification should leave hooks for upgrades. The SIP press for Phase 1 is a Phase-1 commodity press. But the building's foundation depths, ceiling heights, crane bay dimensions, and electrical service should accommodate the larger CLT-finishing equipment, and ultimately the 3D press if Phase 3 reaches it. A Phase-1 facility laid out for Phase 1 only requires a teardown-and-rebuild at Phase 2; a Phase-1 facility laid out for Phase 3 ultimate requires only equipment additions.

The operating agreement should accommodate scope expansion. The current Merge4Ward LLC operating agreement, when it is finalized, should explicitly contemplate (a) future product lines beyond CLT/SIP panels, (b) future entity formation for sister operations under shared ownership, and (c) future conversion to worker cooperative status without losing the strategic-product expansion path. This is an attorney conversation; the language should be drafted with the ZÜBLIN model in mind so the legal structure does not foreclose options that the business model will eventually want to take.

## 4. Recommendations

**One.** Hold ZÜBLIN outreach until Q3 2026 (post-Wells Fargo decision and post-Claymont production start), with the Parcel B carve-out activating earlier if RDC engagement advances. Draft the introductory email now and hold it in queue rather than sending it now — drafting it shapes the thinking and makes the eventual outreach faster.

**Two.** Adopt the ZÜBLIN model logic for Phase 2 and Phase 3 strategic planning. Specifically, expand the Merge4Ward business plan to include (a) prefab-finished-wall product line beginning Year 2, (b) connector distribution business beginning Year 1 (lowest capital cost, fastest revenue, parallel to manufacturing), (c) MOLENO-equivalent multi-story residential system targeting WHA pipeline beginning Year 3, (d) curved-CLT signature capability evaluation beginning Year 4–5.

**Three.** Restructure the Phase 1 site evaluation to consider ultimate Phase-3 footprint, not just Phase-1 footprint. Brief Trevor and Leo on the expanded site requirements before next OED follow-up so the site identification ask reflects the larger eventual scope.

**Four.** Add a strategic-options clause to the Merge4Ward LLC operating agreement covering future product lines, sister entities, and cooperative conversion. Flag for the entity-formation attorney conversation already in progress.

**Five.** Do not put 3D concrete printing under the Merge4Ward roof. If the team wants to pursue it — and there is a real argument for it as a sustainable-construction-stack signal — form a sister entity under shared ownership and share the campus rather than the operating company.

## 5. Things to Watch

A few items worth tracking that affect the analysis above:

**STRABAG public announcements.** If STRABAG announces a North American expansion strategy at any point in the next eighteen months, the calculus changes immediately and outreach becomes urgent rather than scheduled. Their investor relations and corporate communications are worth a quarterly check.

**Stora Enso / KLH / ZÜBLIN US partnership signals.** If any major European CLT producer announces a US partnership or facility, the partnership window for Merge4Ward narrows. Once one European firm has a US partner in the Mid-Atlantic, the others are less interested in adding a second.

**Wilmington OED response to the M4Di OI Tool pilot proposal.** If the pilot moves forward, that is the strongest credibility signal Merge4Ward could carry into a STRABAG conversation — because it demonstrates that the City of Wilmington has institutional alignment with the broader Merge4Ward / M4Di / Aubyn / M4D ecosystem, which means STRABAG would be entering a market with municipal alignment already in place.

**Bensonwood, Entekra, Plant Prefab, and other US prefab firms.** These are the closest US analogs to the ZÜBLIN service-integrated model. Their financial trajectories, customer mix, and capital-raising patterns are useful proxies for what Merge4Ward could realistically achieve at the Phase-2/Phase-3 horizon. Worth a quarterly news check.
