The Delaware Precedent
1939 → 2028
Same instinct.
Different century.

In 1939, DuPont chose a 609-acre site near Seaford, Delaware to build the world's first nylon manufacturing plant. They chose it for water access, rail infrastructure, proximity to markets, and a community hungry for employment. The town became known as "The Nylon Capital of the World." At peak operation, 4,600 Delawareans built their careers there. That plant is now a National Historic Chemical Landmark — and today, INVISTA is exploring its sale.

Merge4Ward proposes to make that site Delaware's next manufacturing landmark — the Mid-Atlantic's first sustainable construction manufacturing campus, producing the CLT structural panels, AAC fire-separation panels, SIP envelope systems, and 3D printed concrete components that the region's affordable housing pipeline needs and currently imports from Illinois and Florida. Same Seaford site. Same Delaware logic. 87 years later.

Section 1 — The Win
What Wilmington gets. What Delaware gets. What it costs to say yes.

Wilmington has a shortage of quality affordable housing and a manufacturing employment base that has declined for two decades. Delaware's Sussex County is watching a 120-person workforce at the INVISTA Seaford plant dwindle from a peak of 4,600 with no plan for what comes next. This proposal addresses all three challenges — without a city or state appropriation — through a layered capital stack assembled from federal grants, New Markets Tax Credits, CDFI debt, and brownfield redevelopment programs that the current site qualifies for on every criterion.

Section 2 — The Building System
Four technologies. One campus. Every major structural component of a Mid-Atlantic affordable housing building under one roof.

The Merge4Ward campus is not a single manufacturing line. It is a phased integration of four sustainable construction technologies — the only facility proposed anywhere in the Mid-Atlantic that produces the complete building system. Phase 1 launches the campus. Phase 2 completes it at Seaford.

LINE 01 · Phase 1 · Now
CLT + SIP Panels

Cross-laminated timber floor decks and structurally insulated panel envelopes. Manufactured in Delaware — eliminating the 2,000-mile Illinois supply chain. $8–15/SF freight savings per project. 2–4 week lead time vs. 16–24 weeks. Validated at Claymont Hybrid.

LINE 02 · Phase 1 · Now
AAC Fire-Separation Panels

Autoclaved aerated concrete party walls — 4-hour fire rating in a single 4" wythe. Currently manufactured only in Florida with no Mid-Atlantic plant. A Delaware facility is the first in the region. Serves every townhouse and mid-rise developer within 500 miles.

FUNCTION · Phase 1 · Now
Modular Wet Wall Assembly

Factory-assembled plumbing walls — PEX supply, DWV stack, pressure-tested — delivered to site on Day 1 of framing. Pulls MEP off the critical path by 3–4 weeks per project. Produced at the campus alongside structural panels.

LINE 03 · Phase 2 · Seaford
3D Printed Concrete

Workshop-based prefabricated concrete components — foundation sections, bond beams, complex geometric elements. Incremental $800K–$2M addition to an existing campus. University of Delaware research partnership anchor. Completes the full building system.

The Proof of Concept Is Already Built: The Claymont Hybrid Townhouses — 1316–1324 N. Claymont Street, Wilmington — completed this summer as the first CLT/SIP hybrid development in the mid-Atlantic region. Build cost: ~$1.25M total (4 units), mid-$300s/unit. ROM validated within target range. ZBA variance secured in New Castle County — the first regulatory precedent for this building type in Delaware. Sell price $200K–$230K/unit. This is not a rendering. It is a completed building.
Section 3 — Workforce + L.E.E.P.
The workforce answer that makes this different from every prior attempt.

Every CLT/SIP manufacturing initiative that preceded Merge4Ward — including Katerra's $2 billion effort — failed in part because trained installers did not exist when the panels arrived. Merge4Ward's answer is structural: L.E.E.P., Inc. (Lead Exceptionally, Empower People), a Wilmington-based 501(c)(3) led by Executive Director Gwenevere Motley, is simultaneously the primary supplier of trainees into the workforce program and the primary employer of certified graduates on its own development pipeline. The training program has a guaranteed employment exit. The manufacturing campus has a guaranteed installer base. That closed loop is the innovation.

L.E.E.P. as Workforce Supplier
Recruiting from Wilmington's LMI Communities — from Day 1

L.E.E.P.'s community relationships in Wilmington's underserved neighborhoods provide the primary trainee pipeline for each Merge4Ward cohort. L.E.E.P. recruits, provides wraparound support (accessing DDDS, DHSS, and workforce block grant funding to keep trainees through completion), and connects trainees to employment on real L.E.E.P. projects — not hypothetical future jobs. Every trainee knows who hires them before they complete the program.

L.E.E.P. as Employer
Certified Graduates Work on L.E.E.P. Projects

Every L.E.E.P. development built with Merge4Ward-produced panels employs Merge4Ward-trained workers — keeping both manufacturing revenue and construction wages in Wilmington's LMI communities. L.E.E.P. gains lower panel costs, faster schedules, and M4Di-documented cost intelligence. In Phase 2, L.E.E.P. has in-house 3DCP-certified operators no other Mid-Atlantic developer can match.

Governor Meyer's Executive Orders
EO #1 + EO #10: Made Real and Visible

Governor Meyer signed Executive Order #1 — creating a working group on youth apprenticeships — within minutes of his inauguration. Executive Order #10 established the Office of Workforce Development. Merge4Ward's registered apprenticeship program, formally recognized through the Delaware Apprenticeship and Training Program (DATP), is the tangible implementation of both executive orders in advanced manufacturing and construction — the exact sectors named in EO #1.

DATP registration unlocks WIOA funding eligibility for every Merge4Ward trainee and makes credential coursework Pell Grant-eligible — turning the training program from grant-dependent to self-sustaining. This costs the Governor's office nothing. It takes approximately 90 days through the Department of Labor.

L.E.E.P. Statewide Expansion: The Seaford Phase 2 campus is the organizational event that justifies L.E.E.P. expanding its 501(c)(3) mission to statewide Delaware — serving Sussex County communities with the same workforce development model proven in Wilmington. DelTech Georgetown (20 miles from Seaford) anchors the Sussex County training program.
