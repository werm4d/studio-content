Section 3B — The Forward Building System
Six product lines. One campus. The complete sustainable construction system.

Merge4Ward is not a panel manufacturer. It is a building system company. The distinction matters for IP, for grant eligibility, for the testing lab strategy, and for the ZUBLIN partnership conversation. The six components below are the full product architecture — Phase 1 launches the first three; Phase 2 at Seaford completes the system.

LINE 01 · Phase 1
4Ward-SIB™ — ThermoSIP Panels

Merge4Ward's proprietary branded panel: Barricade Thermo-Brace SIB face (4-in-1: structural sheathing + air barrier + WRB + R-3/R-5 continuous insulation via BASF Neopor GPS foam) factory-assembled into the 4Ward-SIB system. GREENGUARD Gold certified. Reversible foam orientation for factory conditioning / field install flexibility. TER-evaluated under IRC R703.2 / IBC 1404.2. Eliminates OSB + house wrap + separate CI layer in a single panel. Deployed in Forward Home Program and Claymont hybrid.

LINE 02 · Phase 1
4Ward-Fire™ — Fire-Rated Panels + AAC

Fire-rated separation system for party walls, demising walls, and egress corridors. 4Ward-Fire Panel: Huber EXACOR (ESR-4635) non-combustible MGO face — replacing OSB-based assemblies. AAC (autoclaved aerated concrete) for ground-floor 4-hour rated separation. Merge4Ward-specific ESR for the 4Ward-Fire Panel assembly is a near-term IP target — converting from "approved for use" to "independently listed building product." MagMatrix BMSC 517 SIS (fire-rated + structural + CI in one) on watch-and-activate status pending ICC-ES code acceptance.

LINE 03 · Phase 1 + 2
CLT Components + Connection Hardware

Stress-graded CLT floor decks and roof panels; Merge4Ward as regional distributor and value-added fabricator — custom CNC cutting, connection prep, panel staging. Proprietary connection hardware line: structural timber connectors, hold-downs, panel-to-CMU anchors engineered for the 4Ward-SIB/CLT hybrid system. ZUBLIN Timber partnership under active exploration for Phase 2 — curved CLT, specialty mass timber components, and shared fabrication IP relevant to Riverfront East, civic pavilion, and high-profile IDD campus applications.

FUNCTION · Phase 1
Modular Wet Wall + MEP Assembly

Factory-assembled plumbing walls — PEX supply tree, DWV stack, pressure-tested — delivered to site Day 1 of framing. Pulls MEP off the critical path by 3–4 weeks. CBai MEP Workflow SOP v1 (June 2026) governs soft cost disaggregation and fee compression modeling. At 10-unit Forward Home cluster scale: single factory run, identical assemblies across all units, zero field coordination variance. Scales directly to IDD residential and M-Well Phase 2.

LINE 04 · Phase 2 · Seaford
3D Printed Concrete Building Components

Workshop-based prefabricated concrete: foundation wall sections, bond beams, complex geometric elements — CNC-guided robotic extrusion at the campus, crane-set on-site. Off-site prefabrication model (Citizen Robotics precedent). $800K–$2M incremental Phase 2 addition to the Seaford campus. University of Delaware research partnership anchor — the first campus in Delaware producing real project components for the research record. Unlocks DOE Advanced Manufacturing and NSF construction technology grants unavailable to CLT/SIP manufacturing alone.

PLATFORM · Phase 2 · Seaford
UL / ESR Certified Testing + Evaluation Lab

A Merge4Ward-operated UL listing and ICC-ES ESR/TER evaluation lab embedded in the Seaford campus — leveraging existing QC laboratory infrastructure from the nylon manufacturing facility. Primary target: Merge4Ward-specific ESR for the 4Ward-Fire Panel assembly. Secondary: TER validation for 4Ward-SIB in Delaware code environment. The testing lab is the IP moat: it converts Merge4Ward products from "code-compliant assemblies" to "independently listed building products" — a certification no affiliate or competitor can replicate without physical lab infrastructure. This is what ZUBLIN brings to the table at Phase 2 scale.

Section 4 — The Seaford Opportunity
The world's first nylon plant. Delaware's next manufacturing chapter.

In 1939, DuPont chose a 609-acre site near Seaford, Delaware — close to the Nanticoke River, on the Norfolk Southern rail line, near major markets and raw material supplies — to build the world's first nylon manufacturing plant. The town became known as "The Nylon Capital of the World." The plant employed 4,600 people at its peak. It is a National Historic Chemical Landmark. Today INVISTA, the Koch Industries subsidiary that acquired it in 2004, is exploring a sale. The facility currently employs approximately 120 people. The Seaford Mayor is actively seeking a new owner or tenant who will bring employment back.

The Site — What It Is
750 acres. Rail. River. Ready.

Location: 25876 DuPont Road, Seaford, DE 19973. 750 acres bordered by Nanticoke River (east) and Norfolk Southern Railroad (north).

Infrastructure: Heavy industrial buildings. Overhead crane systems. Existing steam + power infrastructure (on-site power plant). Rail access for bulk material delivery. River access relevant to AAC steam production. EPA corrective action: complete with institutional controls — brownfield redevelopment viable.

Status: INVISTA exploring sale (October 2024). Barclays as exclusive financial advisor. No confirmed buyer. The window is open right now.

The Narrative — Why It Matters
DuPont 1939 → Merge4Ward 2028

DuPont chose Seaford for the same reasons Merge4Ward would: water for manufacturing processes, rail for materials and distribution, proximity to Wilmington headquarters, a community hungry for employment.

DuPont introduced a synthetic material that didn't exist — nylon — and built a world-class manufacturing operation around it. Merge4Ward introduces a sustainable construction system that doesn't exist regionally and builds a manufacturing operation around it.

Same Delaware. Same logic. 87 years apart. That narrative is not marketing copy. It is a true story that travels to national press, Congress, and the Governor's office with no embellishment required.

Why Seaford + Wilmington Together: Wilmington Phase 1 is the right first location — close to the primary market, anchored to Mayor Carney's relationship and the Wells Fargo narrative, accessible to L.E.E.P./DelTech workforce pipeline, activatable faster without brownfield diligence. Seaford Phase 2 is the right scale location — existing heavy industrial infrastructure collapses the capital requirement by $10–20M, brownfield grant stack adds a funding layer unavailable in Wilmington, INVISTA sale creates a specific acquisition window. The two campuses are not alternatives. They are sequence.

Section 5 — L.E.E.P.: Both Pipeline and Beneficiary
The workforce answer that every prior attempt got wrong.

Every CLT/SIP manufacturing attempt that preceded Merge4Ward — including Katerra's $2 billion effort — failed in part because manufactured panels arrived in a market with no trained installers. Training was assumed to follow demand. Demand never arrived because installers were not ready. Merge4Ward's answer is structural, not aspirational: L.E.E.P., Inc. is simultaneously the primary supplier of trainees into the workforce program and the primary employer of certified graduates on its own development pipeline. That closed loop is the innovation.

L.E.E.P. as Supplier — The Training Pipeline

L.E.E.P.'s community relationships and workforce development networks in Wilmington's LMI neighborhoods provide the primary trainee pipeline for each Merge4Ward cohort. L.E.E.P. recruits candidates, provides wraparound support (accessing DDDS, DHSS, and workforce block grant funding to keep trainees in the program through completion), and gives trainees line-of-sight to real employment on real projects — not hypothetical future jobs. L.E.E.P.'s 501(c)(3) structure unlocks wraparound support funding that a for-profit training operator cannot access.

L.E.E.P. as Beneficiary — The Deployment Ground

Every L.E.E.P. development project built with Merge4Ward-produced panels employs Merge4Ward-trained workers — keeping both manufacturing revenue and construction wages in Wilmington's LMI communities. L.E.E.P. gains a sustained competitive advantage as a developer: lower panel costs from local supply, faster schedules from trained crews, and M4Di-documented cost intelligence from the predevelopment stage. In Phase 2, L.E.E.P. has in-house access to 3DCP-certified operators no competing Mid-Atlantic developer can match.

L.E.E.P. Statewide Expansion
From Wilmington to Delaware-Wide

The Seaford Phase 2 campus is the organizational event that justifies L.E.E.P. expanding its 501(c)(3) mission from Wilmington/New Castle County to statewide Delaware. Sussex County has some of Delaware's deepest housing affordability challenges. DelTech Georgetown is 20 miles from Seaford.

The expansion requires an amendment to L.E.E.P.'s Articles of Incorporation — not a new organization, not a new IRS determination letter. Same mission, broader geography. This makes L.E.E.P. eligible for DEDO Strategic Fund grants, DHSS statewide workforce block grants, DNREC Brownfields funding, and DSHA housing finance programs allocated at the state level.

Governor Meyer's registered apprenticeship program designation — under EO #1 and EO #10 — is the specific mechanism that makes every Merge4Ward trainee eligible for WIOA funding and Pell Grants for credential coursework. That turns the training program from grant-dependent to self-sustaining.

Section 7 — Governor Meyer & the Political Opportunity
His first executive order was about apprenticeships. Merge4Ward is the program that makes it real.

Governor Matt Meyer took office January 21, 2025. His first act as Governor — signed within minutes of his inauguration — was Executive Order #1, establishing a working group on youth apprenticeships and career pathways. His tenth executive order established the Office of Workforce Development within the Department of Labor. He has made affordable housing investment a documented first-year priority. He is navigating real tension with Democratic legislators after vetoing SB 63 (construction contractor classification / wage theft) — a veto that labor unions characterized as siding with contractor lobbies over workers.

The Political Gap Merge4Ward Fills: Governor Meyer does not need to reverse his SB 63 position to answer the labor critique. He needs a tangible, visible, documented program that creates manufacturing jobs with registered apprenticeship structures, living wages, and community benefit — in a community that has been watching manufacturing employment disappear for two decades. Merge4Ward is that program. It does not require him to change a legal position. It requires him to endorse an economic development initiative that directly implements his own executive orders.
What Meyer's office gets

A statewide manufacturing narrative that simultaneously serves Wilmington (Mayor Carney's agenda) and Seaford (Sussex County economic development) — the geographic pairing that makes this a gubernatorial story, not just a city story. The Seaford site at a National Historic Chemical Landmark is a press event. Delaware as the first Mid-Atlantic state with regional CLT/SIP and AAC manufacturing is a national story. The IDD neighborhood integration pipeline gives him a healthcare and housing story alongside the manufacturing story. The apprenticeship program directly implements EO #1 and EO #10 in a way he can point to.

The specific ask of his office

Formal recognition of Merge4Ward's registered apprenticeship program under the Delaware Apprenticeship and Training Program (DATP) managed by the Department of Labor. This designation costs nothing, takes approximately 90 days, and makes every Merge4Ward trainee eligible for WIOA funding and Pell Grants. It is the mechanism that makes L.E.E.P.'s wraparound support financially sustainable without perpetual grant dependency. A letter of support for the Seaford site engagement with DEDO is the secondary ask.
